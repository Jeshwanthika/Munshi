# Income-tax slabs and rebate, new regime (Tax Year 2026-27)
Sources: Union Budget 2026 coverage (no change to slabs); Tata Capital and BankBazaar slab tables for FY 2026-27. Checked 9 Nov 2026.

## New regime slabs (default regime)
Up to Rs 4 lakh: nil. Rs 4-8 lakh: 5%. Rs 8-12 lakh: 10%. Rs 12-16 lakh: 15%. Rs 16-20 lakh: 20%. Rs 20-24 lakh: 25%. Above Rs 24 lakh: 30%. A 4% health and education cess is added to the tax. Budget 2026 kept these slabs unchanged from the previous year.

## Rebate: zero tax up to Rs 12 lakh
Resident individuals with taxable income up to Rs 12 lakh get a rebate of up to Rs 60,000, which makes their tax zero under the new regime (old Section 87A, Section 157 of the 2025 Act). Marginal relief applies just above Rs 12 lakh, so the tax cannot exceed the income above Rs 12 lakh. The rebate is not available to non-residents.

## Why this matters for freelancers
With the presumptive scheme, only 50% of receipts is taxable income. A specified professional with up to about Rs 24 lakh of receipts has taxable income up to Rs 12 lakh and can owe zero tax under the new regime.

## Standard deduction
The Rs 75,000 standard deduction is for salary income. Freelance (business/professional) income does not get it.

## Old regime
The old regime has higher rates but allows deductions such as 80C. Taxpayers with business or professional income who switch away from the new regime have limited chances to switch back, so the choice should be made carefully with a CA.
