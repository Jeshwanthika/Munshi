# Presumptive taxation for freelancers (Section 58, Income-tax Act 2025)
Sources: Income Tax Department FAQ on presumptive taxation (incometaxindia.gov.in); taxguru.in analysis of Section 58 vs 44AD/44ADA; payglocal.in Section 58 guide. Checked 9 Nov 2026.

## What changed in 2026
From 1 April 2026 (Tax Year 2026-27) the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act. The old presumptive schemes 44AD (business), 44ADA (profession) and 44AE (transport) are merged into a single Section 58. The rates and limits carry over essentially unchanged.

## Specified professions: 50% of gross receipts
If your work is a specified profession, 50% of gross receipts is treated as your taxable profit and you do not need detailed books. Specified professions (Section 62(4)) are: legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, information technology, company secretary, and others notified by the government. Software developers, web/app developers and IT consultants usually fall under information technology or technical consultancy.
You can always declare a higher profit than 50%. Declaring a lower one generally means keeping books and possibly an audit.

## Receipts limit for professions
The scheme applies if gross receipts are up to Rs 50 lakh, raised to Rs 75 lakh when cash receipts are no more than 5% of total receipts. PayPal, Upwork and bank transfers are not cash, so a freelancer paid this way normally qualifies for the Rs 75 lakh limit.

## Freelancers whose work is a business, not a specified profession
Some freelance work (for example content writing, translation, social-media management, or selling digital products) may be treated as a business rather than a specified profession. The business presumptive scheme deems profit at 6% of receipts received digitally (8% for cash), with a turnover limit of Rs 2 crore, or Rs 3 crore if cash receipts are 5% or less. Under Section 58, a person carrying on a specified profession cannot use the business scheme. Classification can be grey, so confirm it with a CA.

## Who can use it
Resident individuals (and HUFs, partnership firms other than LLPs for the business scheme). Non-residents cannot.
